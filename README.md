# Dice Rolling Circuit – Assembly & Soldering 💡 🎲 

## Overview
A six-sided electronic dice circuit assembled as part of an SFU Engineering Science laboratory course. The circuit uses a touch sensor as the rolling mechanism, triggering a sequence of blinking LEDs before settling on a randomly selected number from 1 to 6, displayed in the arrangement of a traditional die.

This was my first hands-on soldering project, where I was responsible for component assembly, soldering, and basic functional testing.

## Components & Assembly
The circuit consisted of a combination of surface-mount (SMD) and through-hole components, including:

- Through-hole resistors
- SMD and through-hole capacitors
- Through-hole diodes
- Two integrated circuits (ICs)
- Transistor
- Battery holder (wiring)

Following the provided assembly instructions, I identified component placements and soldered each component onto the PCB.

For the SMD components, I used a hand-soldering technique where I first applied a small amount of solder to one PCB pad, reheated it while positioning the component, and then soldered the opposite pad to secure it in place. Through-hole components were inserted into their designated positions and soldered from the opposite side of the board.

## Testing & Troubleshooting
After completing the soldering and assembly, I performed basic checks, including continuity testing, before connecting the circuit to a power supply for functional verification.

During assembly, I initially placed the transistor in the incorrect position. With assistance from the lab engineer, I desoldered and repositioned it correctly. After correcting the placement, I powered the board and verified that the circuit operated as expected.

## What I Learned
- **SMD soldering:** Gained my first experience working with small surface-mount components and learned how to position and secure them accurately using hand-soldering techniques.
- **Through-hole soldering:** Practised soldering larger components and developing consistent solder joints.
- **Component identification:** Became more familiar with identifying component types, orientations, and PCB footprints.
- **Assembly and testing:** Learned the importance of following PCB assembly instructions, checking component placement, and verifying circuit functionality with a power supply.

## Reflection & Areas for Improvement
Overall, I was happy with the quality of my solder joints and took a careful, methodical approach throughout the assembly process. One area I would improve is component alignment and consistency. A couple of SMD capacitors were slightly crooked, and one resistor was not aligned in the same reading direction as the others.

This project gave me a first introduction to PCB assembly and practical soldering, and motivated me to continue developing my hands-on electronics skills.
